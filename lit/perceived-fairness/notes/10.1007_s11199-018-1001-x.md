# Luthar and Ciciolla 2018 (as Sex Roles paper): Invisible household labor, mothers as captains of households

Note on relevance: the paper never measures perceived fairness, the actual split of chores, or time spent on chores. Its exposure is mothers' self-reported "who is mostly responsible" for managing household domains. It bears on the claims only indirectly (C4 and C7 as qualifications, C8 as a measurement remark). It has no bearing on C1, C2, C3, C5, C6 or C9. The text file contains no tables (Tables 1-7 are absent), so numerical results come only from the prose.

## Design
Cross-sectional online self-report survey (secondary analysis of the "Moms as People" dataset). Exposure: three subscales of how many of 13 household-management items the mother answered "Mostly me" to (Household Routines, 4 items; Child Adjustment, 4 items; Household Finances, 5 items), from a three-choice item ("Mostly me", "Mostly my spouse/partner", "Both equally"). Outcomes: partner satisfaction (8 items, alpha .90), life satisfaction, parenting role overload, emptiness. Analysis: hierarchical multiple regression with blocks (demographics; emotional intimacy; physical intimacy; household management entered last as Model 4), reporting change in R2; a supplementary order entering management second. Multiple imputation (20 datasets). Hypotheses (H1a/b, H2a-d) were stated; no preregistration is mentioned (the authors say they "planned, a priori" the partialling of intimacy). Analysis population: 393 imputed mothers; results said to be the same in the complete-case subset (n = 334). Only the mother's report; no partner data, no actual-split measure, no time or task-hours measure.

## Sample
393 U.S. married or partnered mothers with dependent children under 18 at home, drawn from 2,247 women who completed an online survey (2005-2010), recruited by word of mouth, flyers, media reports and lectures, oversampling the well-educated (about half graduate degree, a further quarter college degree). Mostly White, mostly mixed-gender marriages. Up to 14% missing on any variable. Mothers only; no fathers.

## Key results
- Mothers mostly report doing the managing: 70%-88% "Mostly me" on every household-routine item; 53%-80% report finances shared equally.
- Partner satisfaction: after demographics, emotional intimacy and physical intimacy, the management block adds 4% of variance. Responsibility for child adjustment and for finances were significantly negatively associated with partner satisfaction; responsibility for routines was not.
- Life satisfaction (2%) and emptiness (2%): only child-adjustment responsibility was significant; routines and finances not. Parenting role overload: block added 1% and was non-significant.
- Order dependence: entering the management block second gave R2 of .22 for partner satisfaction, falling to 4% when entered last; the intimacy block takes the shared variance (27% when entered after management). The authors read this as management associations being "in part, explained by dimensions of intimacy".
- Total model R2 for partner satisfaction 57%.
- No coefficient values, intervals or standard errors are available in the text (tables absent).

## Limitations
Authors' own: self-report only; upper-middle-class, mostly White U.S. mothers; cross-sectional, correlational, reverse and bidirectional associations possible; no income-share data; no partner reports. They also note low variability in the routines subscale (70%-88% "Mostly me") limits detecting associations.
Mine: the exposure is a perceived allocation of managerial responsibility, not perceived fairness (no item asks whether the allocation is fair) and not the actual split; so it cannot test claims about fairness versus the actual split. "Mostly me" counts discard degree. Subscale reliability is modest (alpha .65 for child adjustment). Partner satisfaction is a mixed scale including resentment and emotional intimacy items, so it overlaps with the intimacy covariates, and intimacy variables are plausibly mediators or consequences of the exposure, making the "controlled" estimate model dependent. Multiple outcomes and subscales with no multiplicity correction. The estimated coefficients are not recoverable from the text.
