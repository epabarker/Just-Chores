# Coundouris & Henry 2026: Dyadic division of household labor and relationship quality, tri-load perspective

## Design
Cross-sectional dyadic survey, preregistered on OSF (hypotheses H1-H4, method, analytic plan). Each partner of a couple completed a separate 10-minute online survey. Two exposure measures per load (physical, cognitive, emotional): (a) "equality", each partner's allocation of 100% of the load across self, partner and others, converted to a woman-minus-man division score for the DRSA; (b) "equity", a single 1-7 item on how fair the division of that load is. Outcome: Dyadic Adjustment Scale total score (0-151), a composite of consensus, satisfaction, cohesion and affectional expression, not a satisfaction-only measure. Analysis: a 2 x 3 within-subjects ANOVA on own perceived contribution (gender x load type) for H1, then six dyadic response surface analyses (DRSA, built on the actor-partner interdependence model) of polynomial terms of own and partner ratings on each partner's relationship quality. The estimand is the shape of the surface of relationship quality over the pair (own rating, partner rating), including congruence and discrepancy, not the effect of fairness controlling for the split. No mediation analysis, no regression of fairness on the split, no comparison referents or justifications, and no task-level predictors were measured or reported. Both exposures are self-reports by the same respondent as the outcome; no independent measure of the actual split exists.

## Sample
235 heterosexual cisgender cohabiting dyads (470 people) recruited through Prolific, UK-based, aged 19-78, mean relationship length 17.10 years (SD 12.22); 67.66% married, 69.79% with children, 76.60% dual-earner. Of 250 invited couples, 15 were dropped (non-response 5, unfinished 7, same-sex 2, separated 1). Preregistered minimum 175 couples, powered for r = .21 at 80%. Demographics, means and correlations are in Supplementary 1-4, which were not available in this text.

## Key results
- C6: Women self-reported doing more than their male partners in every load (Table 1: physical 55.35 vs 47.19, cognitive 65.31 vs 41.71, emotional 57.96 vs 44.31; collapsed M = 59.54 vs 44.40). The two partners' own-share means sum to 102.54 (physical), 107.02 (cognitive), 102.27 (emotional) and 103.94 collapsed. These sums are my arithmetic on the reported means; the authors do not compute them or discuss over-claiming. Because participants could also allocate a share to others (children, external help), the true two-partner sum is expected to be at or below 100, so sums above 100 are consistent with each partner over-claiming. The sum is of group means over unpaired self-reports, not a within-couple test, and no CI is given.
- C4: Equality (reported split) discrepancy, DRSA a4, was significant and negative for 5 of 6 gender x load cells (physical women -1.10**, men -.76*; emotional women -.74**, men -.56*; cognitive men -.47*), while the equity (fairness) a4 was non-significant in every cell (e.g. physical women 1.06 (.80), men 1.08 (.86)). The authors say so and note the single-item fairness measure may be unreliable. This is the opposite direction to C4, but the estimand differs (partner discrepancy in reported split vs in fairness, not own fairness vs split), the split here is self-perceived, not actual, and the outcome is the DAS total. At the level of own-perception effects the picture is mixed: some equity polynomial terms are significant (women cognitive b1 .86*, b3 -.70*; men cognitive b2 -1.10***, b5 1.34***) and some equality terms (women physical b3 -.41**; men physical b2 .32***). No standardised comparison of the two predictors' strength is made.
- C9: Over-benefit is not identified. The fairness item is a 1-7 not-at-all-fair to extremely-fair scale with no direction of unfairness, so over-benefited and under-benefited partners cannot be separated. The nearest result is on the equality measure: men who reported that their female partner did more of the physical load had higher relationship quality (a linear actor effect), the opposite direction to the claim, though it is a split-based, not fairness-based, contrast and a term in a quadratic model.
- C8: The authors state that equality and equity were each a single item, and that prior household-labor fairness research "often" used similar single-item ratings, citing Hiekel & Ivanova 2023 and Jansen et al. 2015 (two examples, not a survey of the field).
- C1, C2, C3, C5, C7: not addressed. The paper does not model fairness as an outcome, or comparison referents, accepted justifications, mediation through fairness, or which chores are done versus time.

## Limitations
Authors' own: the equity item is a broad single question with no reliability estimate; heterosexual cisgender couples only; couples with and without children pooled with no group comparisons; single items for each load rather than task-level measures; a broad relationship-quality measure; cross-sectional, so no direction or causality; the discussion (p.29) calls for multi-item fairness scales separating societal-level fairness from responsibility-based justification. Mine: all measures are same-source self-reports with randomised order only; the sample is a UK Prolific panel of long-partnered couples; many parameters are tested (six DRSAs, many coefficients) with no multiplicity correction stated for the DRSAs (Bonferroni applied only to ANOVA contrasts); equality scores are compositional (a partners-only renormalisation was run in the ANOVA as a robustness check, footnote 1); the paper reports standardised coefficients with unusual SEs (e.g. .001) in Table 2, so effect-size comparison across cells is hard; DAS total conflates consensus (which itself includes household-task agreement) with satisfaction, so the outcome partly overlaps the exposure; the item for "equality" is a perceived split, not an observed one, so over-claiming and the actual split cannot be separated. The text was read from a pdf of an accepted, unedited manuscript ("Article in Press"), so numbers may change before publication. Table 2's text extraction puts one cell per line, so which column a cell belongs to was taken from the order of the table.
