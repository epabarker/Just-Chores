# Major 1993: Gender, Entitlement, and the Distribution of Family Labor

## Design
Narrative, theory-building review (Journal of Social Issues, 49(3), 141-159). No original data, no estimand, no model, no preregistration. It proposes that the "paradoxical contentment" of wives with an unequal split of family work is explained by gender differences in entitlement, which in turn come from (1) comparison standards (social, normative, feasibility, self-comparisons), (2) justifications (procedural fairness, justice rules, breadwinner exchange) and (3) wants and values. Every empirical statement about family work is reported from other studies (Biernat & Wortman 1991, Buunk & VanYperen, Ferree, Hochschild, Pleck, Berk and others). The three mechanisms are hypotheses; the paper itself tests none of them.

## Sample
None. The author notes that the cited research is mostly North American samples (with Buunk and colleagues, Netherlands, as exceptions) and from the latter half of the 20th century.

## Key results
- Aggregate-level pattern reported from reviews and surveys: wives do two to three times as much family work as husbands, but most studies find they do not perceive it as unfair (e.g. 62% of wives in Biernat & Wortman 1991 felt husbands did a satisfactory amount). This is a marginal pattern and gives no variance explained, so it only loosely relates to C1.
- Comparison referents (C2): the one quantitative statement is the VanYperen & Buunk (1991) correlation, which was higher for same-sex ("referential") comparisons with satisfaction than for partner ("relational") comparisons with satisfaction. The outcome is relationship satisfaction, not perceived fairness of the split, and no adjustment for the actual split is reported here. The rest is conjecture, illustrated by Hochschild's case of one wife.
- Justifications (C3): the author says that no study has examined the procedural "voice" justification in intimate relationships. The breadwinner justification is supported only by Ferree 1991a, reported second-hand, with an outcome of felt entitlement to help and no adjustment for the split reported.
- Nothing bears on C4, C5, C6, C7, C8 or C9.

## Limitations
Authors' own: the analysis is culturally specific (North American), dated to the latter 20th century, and focused on contented wives, which may hide discontented ones. Mine: it is a narrative review with no systematic search, no effect sizes, no intervals, and no adjustment for the actual split in any cited result. Terms (entitlement, satisfaction, fairness) are used interchangeably across studies. The text was read from a scan (all 19 pages are image pages; a machine-read text layer), so quotes may carry misread characters. `library.py info` crashed on this paper because the `abstract` version has no source.pdf, so the manifest of version `unknown` was read directly.
