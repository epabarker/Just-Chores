# Gager 1998: Valued outcomes, justifications and comparison referents in perceptions of fairness among dual-earner couples

## Design
Qualitative, cross-sectional, open-ended interviews (45 to 90 minutes) with both spouses of 25 dual-earner couples, interviewed separately, plus a short questionnaire that included the single NSFH fairness item. Nonprobability purposive sampling. Descriptive and interpretive: no regression, no estimand, no statistical control for the actual split, no preregistration. The author states the aim as to "identify and describe how valued outcomes, justifications, and choice of comparison referents mediate between inequality in the division of household labor and perceived fairness", as a first step toward survey questions. The paper also gives a descriptive comparison with an NSFH dual-earner subsample (percentage of spouses spending no time per task; fairness response shares).

## Sample
50 respondents (25 married couples), recruited by letter through day care centres in Philadelphia (one private centre) and Minneapolis/St. Paul (five centres of a national corporate network); October 1994 to May 1995. Inclusion: married, both worked at least 30 hours a week, at least one child aged 10 or under at the centre. Only respondents who replied to the contact letter were included, so selection is on willingness to respond; the response rate is not given. 46 of 50 non-Hispanic White; about half middle class, 14 upper middle class; 17 of 25 couples had household income over $50,000. Wives averaged 37.8 paid hours a week, husbands 41.3. No attrition reported.

## Key results
- Fairness distribution (single item and interview, p.11): 12 wives and 14 husbands judged the split somewhat unfair to the wife, 11 wives and 9 husbands fair to both; fewer than in the NSFH said "fair".
- Comparison referents (C2): the author reports that wives who could discuss household setups with peers, or who found referents worse off, were more likely to say the split was fair. This is an interview-based association in 25 couples, with no adjustment for the actual split, no counts, and no effect size. Parental models were reported as not determining perceptions.
- Justifications (C3): spouses offered many justifications (women are more efficient, husband earns more or works longer hours), but the most overburdened wives could not justify their outcomes and called the split unfair. Reported qualitatively, with no counts. The link between justification and fairness appears to vary with the actual burden, which is the opposite of an independent effect net of the split.
- Marital quality (C4, secondary): wives with lower marital quality were more likely to see the split as unfair; the author says this link is documented in NSFH work (refs listed under Follow up). No comparison with the strength of the split-satisfaction association.
- Measurement (C8): the fairness measure here is the single NSFH item; the author says most prior work relies on NSFH survey data with fairness as the outcome. Hawkins et al. (1995) is described as the exception, which could not use some social-psychological measures because of low consistency reliability.
- Predictive power of objective variables (C1, C7): the author states, from refs, that time and tasks have had little success predicting fairness in national survey data. Not tested here.
- Not addressed: C5 (mediation of split to satisfaction by fairness), C6 (over-claiming; spouses estimated percentages of total time for each of 11 tasks, but the sum is not reported), C9 (over-benefited partner). The paper is about under-benefited wives and does not report satisfaction by benefit status.

## Limitations
- Authors' own: nonprobability purposive sample of 25 couples, mostly White and middle class and recruited through day care centres; the author says the work is a first step to inform survey design and to generate hypotheses to be tested quantitatively. One of two targeted diverse centres yielded one couple.
- Read from a scan: this version is a vision model's (MinerU2.5) reading of a scanned paper (`unknown-ocr`), so quotes are machine-read and could carry OCR errors; no table or figure carries a result.
- Ours: no statistical control, no effect sizes or counts for the referent and justification findings, so no extract can support a claim of prediction net of the actual split. The author's interpretation of interview accounts (for example that husbands "may have invoked the do-nothing dad referent to reduce their cognitive dissonance") is a reading, not a test. The direction of causation between referents or justifications and fairness is untested; respondents may choose referents and justifications after forming a view of fairness. Selection into responding is unknown. The C1, C4 and C7 passages are secondary reporting of other studies' results.
