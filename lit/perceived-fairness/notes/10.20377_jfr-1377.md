# Adriaans, Bohmeier & Sauer 2026: Perceived fairness of division of labor and relationship satisfaction

## Design
Cross-sectional, observational research note (JFR 38, 348-362). Linear regression (OLS, robust SEs, FReDA survey weights) of single-item relationship satisfaction (0-10) on a 5-category perceived-fairness item with "roughly my fair share" as reference, plus controls. Separate models for women and men, with coefficient equality tested across models (chi-square). Estimand: between-person adjusted association, not a causal effect; the authors say longitudinal data are needed for causal ordering. No preregistration stated. Listwise deletion. The fairness item covers housework and paid work jointly, so it is not a chore-only fairness measure. The actual split of chores enters only as a control (a mean index of seven household tasks); no mediation analysis and no model without the fairness item is reported, so the paper does not test a mediation or variance-share question.

## Sample
FReDA wave W1A (2021, Germany), respondents aged 18-49 cohabiting with a different-sex partner. 22,048 respondents in the wave, 12,107 after restrictions, N = 11,012 after listwise deletion (6,029 women, 4,983 men). Individual respondents, not couples; no partner data used. Appendix (Table A1, Figure A1) is not in the text file.

## Key results
Full sample (Model 1, Table 2), relative to "roughly my fair share": much more -1.381***, a bit more -.443***, a bit less -.390***, much less -.279 (SE .201, n.s.). Women: much less -1.862** (SE .625, few cases); men: much less .033 (n.s.). Index of household tasks (controls): .365*** full sample, .523*** women, .129 men (n.s.); scale not described in the text, coefficients unstandardised and on a different scale from the fairness dummies, so not directly comparable. R2 = .093 full, .144 women, .055 men (all predictors together; no decomposition of the fairness contribution).
Differences between gender models: a bit more (chi2=9.80, p=.002); much less (chi2=8.38, p=.004).

## Limitations
Authors': between-person design, causal ordering unclear (conflict may raise unfairness perception); Germany only, ages 18-49; low cell counts for women who do much less than fair share. Mine: single self-report item for fairness and for satisfaction, both from the same respondent (common-method and reverse-causation risk); joint paid+unpaid labor item, not chores alone; "over-benefited" is inferred from the "less than fair share" categories, with no equity measure of the actual split; chore index is one self-report mean index of seven tasks, with no hours and no partner report; the paper does not examine comparison referents, justifications, over-claiming, task type or mediation. Many comparisons with no multiplicity adjustment. Appendix not available to read.
