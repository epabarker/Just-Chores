# DeMaris & Longmore 1996: Ideology, power and equity in the perception of fairness of household labor

## Design
Cross-sectional survey analysis (National Survey of Families and Households, 1987-88). Separate logistic regressions for husbands and wives of the log odds that the respondent sees housework as "unfair to the wife" (versus fair to both or unfair to the husband), with an OLS version on the five-level item as a robustness check. Predictors are the relative contribution to routine housework (logged husband/wife hours ratio), ideology (sex-role egalitarianism, endorsement of housework sharing), wives' alternatives (income, education, hours employed, health) and equity terms (logged husband/wife ratios of sporadic housework hours, mealtime with children, child socialization, income, paid hours, health; dummies for relative education and second jobs), plus controls. Hours of housework are the respondent's own report of both partners' hours. The estimand is the association of each factor with perceived unfairness net of the others; it is not causal, and the authors say the causal order between reported hours and perceived fairness may be reciprocal. Relationship satisfaction is not an outcome. Comparison referents are not measured; the "justification" construct is operationalised only through objective compensating contributions (equity) and ideology.

## Sample
2,153 couples from the NSFH with a child aged 5-18, married or cohabiting, opposite-sex, no one in the armed forces, at least one partner employed full-time and the partner questionnaire returned. Analysis N is 2,109 husbands and 2,096 wives (missing dependent variable excluded); 2,054 couples with both partners' answers for the Table 1 comparison. Missing predictor values were mean-imputed; imputation was checked and had no detectable effect. Analyses weighted for oversampling.

## Key results
- Perceived fairness is a single 5-point NSFH item (C8).
- The multivariate models have McKelvey-Zavoina R2 of .21 (husbands) and .16 (wives). This is the whole model (routine and sporadic split, ideology, equity, controls), not the split alone (C1).
- Routine-hours ratio: odds ratio .767 per unit (husbands) and .800 (wives); controlling for it, the wife's absolute routine hours had no effect.
- Equity terms (sporadic hours, child care, health, paid hours) predict perceived unfairness net of the routine ratio; the wife's sporadic ratio had a significantly stronger effect than the routine ratio (p<.002) for wives only (C3, C7).
- Ideology (endorsement of housework sharing) predicts perceived fairness net of the split (C3, weakly).
- Wives' alternatives (human capital) did little; the authors found "relatively little" support for power.
- No test of self-reported shares summing to more than 100 percent (C6); footnote 2 assumes over-reporting is random across genders.

## Limitations
Authors' own: hours are reports, and perceptions of fairness may distort recall of hours (non-recursive link); no data on who plans and supervises chores; no data on task preferences; no data on access to alternative partners. Ours: cross-sectional and one wave; the fairness measure is a single item; the "equity" terms are objective ratios and do not capture which justifications the respondent actually accepts, so C3 is only indirectly addressed; "routine" versus "sporadic" is a split by task type but both enter as hours ratios, so chore type is not separated cleanly from time; R2 is a pseudo-R2 for a latent variable on a dichotomised outcome; many equity coefficients are significant at p<.05 only, with no multiplicity adjustment; most reported effects are only shown when significant. The paper was read from a scan (the text layer is machine-read, with some misread characters); the quotes below copy the text as it stands.

## Follow up
- Lennon & Rosenfield 1994, "Relative Fairness and the Division of Housework: The Importance of Options", American Journal of Sociology 100:506-31 (reports social comparison among powerless wives and options; closely bears on C2).
- Blair & Johnson 1992, "Wives' Perceptions of the Fairness of the Division of Household Labor: The Intersection of Housework and Ideology", Journal of Marriage and the Family 54:570-81.
- Thompson 1991, "Family Work: Women's Sense of Fairness", Journal of Family Issues 12:181-96.
- Ward 1993, "Marital Happiness and Household Equity in Later Life", Journal of Marriage and the Family 55:427-38 (may bear on C4 and C9).
- Benin & Agostinelli 1988, "Husbands' and Wives' Satisfaction with the Division of Labor", Journal of Marriage and the Family 50:349-61 (satisfaction with the division; C4).
