# Ruppanner 2017: Division of housework and his and her view of housework fairness, a typology of Swedish couples

## Design
Cross-sectional, exploratory, descriptive-associational. Step 1: latent class analysis (Stata LCA plugin) on three couple-level indicators: his share of housework hours (4 bands), her perceived fairness (5-point recode), and agreement between partners on fairness. Step 2: each couple is assigned to its modal class and class membership is regressed on predictors by multinomial logistic regression, reference class group 4 (equal sharing / agree fair). Predictors are mapped to distributive justice theory: outcome (actual share) enters only as an indicator of the classes, comparative referents (childhood housework socialization) and justifications (gender ideology, work status, relative income, education). No preregistration, no causal identification strategy. The estimand is association with membership of a share-by-fairness-by-agreement class, not with perceived fairness conditional on the actual split. Relationship satisfaction is not measured.

## Sample
Young Adult Panel Study (YAPS), Sweden, 2009 wave; cohorts born 1968, 1972, 1976, 1980; Swedish-born with Swedish, Turkish or Polish-born parents. Of 1,528 married or cohabiting respondents, 1,074 (70%) had partners who also answered; 1,020 couples remain after excluding same-sex couples (Table 2 N=1,020; Table 3 header says n=1,026). Housework is self-reported hours for groceries/cooking/dishes, laundry/clothes, cleaning; partner report fills in when self-report is missing; remaining gaps filled with the same-sex mean.

## Key results
- Six latent classes. Modal class is equal sharing / agree fair (32.7%, "one-third"). Class 5 (25.5%) has semi-equal shares but she says unfair and he says fair.
- Fairness is a single five-category self-report item per partner; only her item and agreement enter the LCA.
- Childhood socialization (her mother did most of the housework) predicts membership in classes 2, 3 and 5 relative to class 4 (Table 3: 1.189 ***, 0.713 ^, 0.481 *), including class 5 where the split is semi-equal. Authors read this as women socialized in traditional homes viewing housework as unfair "regardless of the actual division".
- Justifications give mixed results: "she more egalitarian" raises class 3 membership (0.663 *, class 3 = both partners say unfair to her); women's lower employment and income do not "justify" inequality as fair; for class 1 no distributive-justice predictor works (only presence of a child, 0.601 **).
- Table 3 gives only coefficients and significance stars on class membership, no standard errors, intervals or effect sizes on a fairness scale.

## Limitations
Authors: exploratory; the cut points for the share bands matter for class 3 (a wider "equal" band dissolved it); not longitudinal, so classes may be transitory (life-course stage); "without more robust empirical evaluation no clear conclusions can be drawn". Reader: the outcome is a latent class defined partly by the actual split, so the models do not hold the split constant while predicting fairness; modal-class assignment ignores classification uncertainty in the second step; predictors are compared with the reference class 4 only, so a coefficient mixes differences in split and in fairness; no variance-explained statistics, so claim C1 cannot be assessed; her fairness item is the only fairness input to the LCA (his fairness enters only via agreement); partners' education and socialization are proxy-reported; no satisfaction, over-claiming, chore-type or mediation analysis. Inconsistent sample size (1,020 vs 1,026).
